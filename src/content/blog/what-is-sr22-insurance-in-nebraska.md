## What Is SR22 Insurance?

SR22 is not actually a type of insurance. Instead, it's a certificate of financial responsibility that proves you carry at least the minimum required liability insurance to drive legally. The SR22 is filed by your insurance company with the Nebraska Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV).  

This certificate serves as a guarantee to the state that you have the necessary liability coverage to compensate for damages in the event of an accident.  

### Nebraska’s Minimum Insurance Requirements

Nebraska law mandates the following minimum coverage levels for liability insurance, which your SR22 filing will verify that you have:

* **$25,000**

   for bodily injury per person  
* **$50,000**

   for bodily injury per accident  
* **$25,000**

   for property damage  

Your SR22 acts as proof that these coverage levels are met.  

## Who Needs SR22 Insurance in Nebraska?

SR22 insurance is typically required for high-risk drivers who have been involved in certain driving-related offenses. Common reasons why someone might need SR22 insurance in Nebraska include:  

* **Driving Under the Influence (DUI):**

   A DUI conviction can lead to license suspension and requires filing an SR22 to reinstate your driving privileges.  
* **Driving Without Insurance:**

   If you’re caught operating a vehicle without at least the minimum liability coverage, you may be required to file an SR22.  
* **Reckless Driving:**

   This charge can result in mandatory SR22 filing, depending on the severity of the offense.  
* **License Suspension or Revocation:**

   If your driving privileges have been suspended due to excessive traffic violations, an accident while uninsured, or failure to pay fines, you may be required to secure an SR22 to reinstate your license.  

## How SR22 Insurance Works in Nebraska

### Filing the SR22

When you’re required to get SR22 insurance in Nebraska, the process typically involves these steps:

1. **Contact an Insurance Provider:**

   Not all insurers offer SR22 coverage, so it’s important to confirm their ability to handle SR22 filings.  
2. **Pay the Filing Fee:**

   The insurance company will charge a filing fee, usually ranging from $15 to $25.  
3. **Insurer Files the Form:**

   Once you’ve secured the appropriate insurance policy and paid the fee, the company electronically files the SR22 with the Nebraska DMV.  
4. **Maintain Continuous Coverage:**

   Nebraska laws require that the SR22 remain active for a specified duration. If your policy lapses, the insurance provider will notify the DMV, potentially resulting in another suspension of your driving privileges.  

### Duration of SR22 Requirement

The period you need SR22 insurance varies based on the type and severity of your violation. However, in Nebraska, the standard requirement is typically **three years**. You must maintain uninterrupted coverage during this time.  

If you switch insurance companies or fail to renew your policy, your SR22 status could be canceled, and your license may be suspended again.  

### Non-Owner SR22 Insurance

For Nebraskans who don’t own a vehicle but are still required to file an SR22, a non-owner SR22 policy can fulfill the state’s insurance requirements.  

Non-owner SR22 insurance provides liability coverage when you drive a vehicle you don’t own, such as a rental car or a friend's vehicle. This option is ideal for those who drive infrequently or don’t want to purchase a traditional auto policy.

## FAQs About SR22 Insurance in Nebraska

### How Do I Know If I Need SR22 Insurance?

You’ll likely receive a notification from the Nebraska DMV or a court order specifying this requirement as part of your reinstatement process.

### Can I Drive Right After Getting SR22 Insurance?

You can legally drive once your SR22 certificate is filed and approved by the Nebraska DMV. Make sure you’ve also met all other reinstatement requirements, like paying fines or completing necessary classes.

### What Happens If My SR22 Insurance Lapses?

If your SR22 policy is canceled or lapses, your insurer is required to notify the DMV. This could lead to another suspension of your license, and you may need to restart the SR22 process.
